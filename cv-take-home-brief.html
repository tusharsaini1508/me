01

The problem

People photograph paper receipts with a phone: crooked, badly lit, glare across the total, half the frame is the table.

Build a small system that takes one of those photos and returns structured, trustworthy data — or honestly refuses when it can't.

"Trustworthy" is the operative word. A pipeline that guesses total = 12.00 on an unreadable photo is worse for us than one that says "I can't read this, retake it." Your scoring reflects that — see section 05.

02

What you must build

2.1 — A capture-quality gate

Before you attempt any extraction, decide whether the image is usable. At minimum detect:

  • Blur / out-of-focus
  • Severe under- or over-exposure, and glare over text regions
  • Insufficient resolution
  • Document too small in the frame, or cut off at the edges

When the image fails, return an actionable message ("Too blurry — hold steady and retake"), not a generic error.

2.2 — Document detection and rectification

Locate the receipt in the photo and produce a flat, deskewed, cropped image of just the receipt. Perspective correction is required — a naive centre-crop will not pass.

2.3 — Field extraction

From the rectified image, extract:

FieldTypeFormat
merchant_namestringas printed
transaction_datestringYYYY-MM-DD
total_amountnumber2 decimal places
currencystringISO 4217, e.g. INR, USD

2.4 — Per-field confidence and abstention

Every field carries a confidence score and a needs_review flag. Your thresholds are your own choice — but you must justify them in your report. Abstaining is free; being confidently wrong is expensive.

2.5 — Two interfaces

a) Streamlit app. Upload an image, see the quality verdict, the rectified crop, the extracted JSON, and the fields flagged for review. Deployed and publicly reachable.

b) A headless function, in src/pipeline.py:

def extract(image_path: str) -> dict:
    ...

returning exactly this shape:

{
  "quality": {
    "pass": true,
    "issues": []
  },
  "fields": {
    "merchant_name":    {"value": "Cafe Nine", "confidence": 0.91, "needs_review": false},
    "transaction_date": {"value": "2026-03-14", "confidence": 0.88, "needs_review": false},
    "total_amount":     {"value": 480.00,       "confidence": 0.42, "needs_review": true},
    "currency":         {"value": "INR",        "confidence": 0.95, "needs_review": false}
  },
  "processing_ms": 1840
}

When quality.pass is false, issues must be a non-empty list of strings and every field value may be null. We run our own held-out images through this function, so match the schema exactly.

2.6 — Your own evaluation harness

Assemble a labelled test set of at least 12 receipt photos that you shoot or source yourself. Include deliberately hard cases — this is part of the assessment. Store ground truth as JSON or CSV in the repo.

Then ship a runnable evaluator:

python eval.py --data test/

It should print per-field accuracy, abstention rate, and the trust score defined in section 05.

2.7 — A short report

REPORT.md, roughly one page:

  • Your metrics from eval.py
  • Three failure cases, with the image, what went wrong, and why
  • Where your thresholds came from
  • What you would do with another week
  • Which AI coding tools you used and for what — see section 04
03

Constraints

  • Python 3.10+, CPU-only. No GPU at inference.
  • No paid or hosted vision/OCR APIs. Google Vision, AWS Textract, Azure Document Intelligence, and hosted LLM vision endpoints are all out. Open-source OCR is fine: Tesseract, EasyOCR, PaddleOCR, docTR, or your own approach.
  • Deployed on Streamlit Community Cloud (free) or an equivalent free host. Cold start under ~60s; single image processed in under ~10s.
  • Public GitHub repo, MIT licensed, with requirements.txt — and packages.txt if you need apt packages such as tesseract-ocr or libgl1.
  • Pinned dependency versions.

Note on the memory ceiling. Streamlit's free tier gives you about 1 GB of RAM. Some OCR stacks will not fit. Choosing a model that survives the deployment target is part of the engineering problem — we will not treat "it worked locally" as working.

04

Ground rules on AI assistants

Use them. Copilot, Claude, Cursor, whatever you normally work with — that is how the job actually gets done.

Two conditions:

  • Disclose what you used and for what in REPORT.md.
  • You will walk us through your code live for 20 minutes. Be able to explain and defend any line of it, including why you rejected the alternatives.

Code you cannot explain will be treated as code you did not write.

05

How we score

Weighting is roughly:

Pipeline correctness & robustness35%
Evaluation rigour & honest error analysis25%
Code quality20%
Streamlit app usability10%
Deployment & reproducibility10%
Total100%

Extraction accuracy is measured with a trust score across all fields on our held-out set:

Correct and not flagged+1
Flagged for review (right or wrong)0
Wrong and not flagged−1

Field matching: merchant_name case-insensitive with fuzzy match ≥ 0.85; transaction_date exact; total_amount exact to 2 decimals; currency exact.

A calibrated system that abstains on the hard 30% will beat an overconfident one that scores higher on raw accuracy. That is intentional.

We are looking for engineering judgment under a deadline: what you chose to build, what you deliberately left out, and how well you know where your own system breaks. A modest pipeline you understand completely beats an ambitious one you don't.

Optional stretch — only if the core is genuinely finished

  • Learned corner detection instead of contour heuristics
  • Multiple receipts in one frame
  • Line-item extraction
  • A latency budget with measurements per stage

Skipping every stretch item and nailing the core is a strong submission. Half-finished stretch work on a shaky core is not.
